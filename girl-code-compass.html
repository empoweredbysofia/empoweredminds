What schools may already be noticing

Some concerns are highly visible. Others are easily missed until pressure has already built.

Repeated behaviour incidents

Escalation, impulsive responses or difficulty recovering after conflict.

Persistent lateness

Increasing difficulty arriving, settling or engaging with the school day.

Conflict with staff

Communication breakdowns, defensiveness or repeated misunderstandings.

Friendship difficulties

Peer conflict, exclusion, people-pleasing or pressure to fit in.

Lesson avoidance

Leaving lessons, reduced participation or recurring visits to pastoral staff.

Quiet withdrawal

Masking, disengagement or struggling without drawing adult attention.

Anxiety and perfectionism

High achievement alongside intense internal pressure and fear of mistakes.

Growing pastoral demand

Increasing staff time without a clear, structured intervention pathway.

Different behaviours can affect learning, relationships and school culture.
Early understanding and targeted support can prevent concerns becoming more entrenched.

Behind every presentation is a story

Visible

“Always in detention.”

She may be reacting before she has the language or strategies to communicate what she needs.

Less visible

“The quiet one.”

She may be masking anxiety, fear of getting things wrong or a growing sense of disconnection.

Hidden pressure

“The perfectionist.”

Achievement can coexist with intense self-criticism, people-pleasing and emotional exhaustion.

A structured journey from awareness to healthier action

Five connected stages, not five isolated wellbeing activities.

WEEK 1 · UNDERSTAND

Recognising My Patterns

Identity, emotions, triggers, protective responses and strengths.

WEEK 2 · FIND MY VOICE

Confidence, Voice and Needs

Self-belief, appropriate communication and recognising trusted support.

WEEK 3 · CONNECT

Relationships and Boundaries

Peer dynamics, conflict, healthy boundaries, repair and belonging.

WEEK 4 · RESPOND

From Reaction to Response

Recognising escalation, practising regulation and making reflective choices.

WEEK 5 · MOVE FORWARD

My Compass Forward

Consolidating learning and creating a realistic continuation plan.

Structured enough for schools. Personal enough to matter.

The cohort is intentionally limited to five students, enabling meaningful discussion, responsive facilitation and clear links between learning and real school experiences.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the programme for?

Girls aged 11–16 who may benefit from targeted support with confidence, emotional awareness, relationships, communication, engagement or emerging behavioural and attendance concerns.

How many students can take part?

A maximum of five students in one consistent cohort.

Does the programme guarantee improvements in behaviour or attendance?

No single intervention can guarantee those outcomes. The programme develops understanding, communication and reflective skills that can support healthier participation within a wider school plan.

Where is it delivered?

In person within the commissioning school.
